# INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

# Market Intelligence Platform

---

# Purpose

This document defines how information should be prioritized throughout the platform.

Good design starts with good information hierarchy.

The objective is not to display the maximum amount of information.

The objective is to help users understand the market as quickly as possible.

Every page should answer a small number of important questions before exposing additional detail.

---

# Information Hierarchy

Every piece of information belongs to one of four levels.

## Level 1 — Critical

Critical information should be visible immediately.

Users should never scroll to see it.

Examples

- Market Mood
- Today's Headline
- Confidence
- Importance
- Top Drivers
- Biggest Risk
- Biggest Opportunity

Maximum reading time:

10 seconds

---

## Level 2 — Important

Important information explains the critical information.

Examples

- Economic Ripple
- Market Movers
- Key Companies
- Key Industries
- AI Key Points

Maximum reading time:

30 seconds

---

## Level 3 — Supporting

Supporting information provides evidence.

Examples

- Latest Dispatches
- Research Suggestions
- Supporting Headlines
- Related Articles
- AI Narrative

Users should only reach this level after understanding Levels 1 and 2.

---

## Level 4 — Reference

Reference information is for exploration.

Examples

- Historical Events
- Macro Charts
- Full News Feed
- Company Financials
- SEC Filings
- Economic Indicators
- Complete AI Reports

Reference information should never compete visually with critical information.

---

# Dashboard Principles

The dashboard is not a report.

The dashboard is a decision-support workspace.

Users should understand today's market before reading today's news.

---

# Progressive Disclosure

Always reveal information gradually.

Summary

↓

Evidence

↓

Details

↓

Reference

Never expose every detail at once.

---

# Above the Fold

The first screen should answer:

- What is today's market mood?
- Why is the market moving?
- What is the biggest risk?
- What is the biggest opportunity?
- How is India affected?

Nothing else should compete with these questions.

---

# Below the Fold

Below the fold should contain:

- Market Movers
- Research Suggestions
- Latest Dispatches
- Supporting Articles

These sections provide additional context rather than the primary story.

---

# Card Priority

Cards should appear in this order.

1. Hero
2. Market Snapshot
3. Drivers
4. Economic Ripple
5. Market Movers
6. Continue Research
7. Latest Dispatches

If new cards are introduced, they must justify why they deserve higher priority than an existing card.

---

# Reading Flow

Users should naturally follow this flow.

Today's Story

↓

Why it happened

↓

Who is affected

↓

What should I watch

↓

Read supporting evidence

This flow should remain consistent throughout the platform.

---

# Page Responsibilities

## Today

Explain today's market.

---

## Companies

Explain one company.

Not today's news.

---

## Industries

Explain one industry.

Not individual headlines.

---

## Macro

Explain macroeconomic conditions.

Not company-specific events.

---

## Research

Help users investigate topics more deeply.

Not duplicate dashboard information.

---

# Content Rules

Every paragraph should justify its existence.

If the same information can be communicated using:

- a badge,
- a chip,
- a progress indicator,
- a timeline,
- a visual chain,

prefer the visual representation.

---

# Navigation Philosophy

Navigation should represent user questions.

Today

"What happened today?"

Companies

"What is happening with this company?"

Industries

"Which industries are changing?"

Macro

"What macro forces matter?"

Research

"What should I investigate next?"

---

# Product Rule

The dashboard should reduce thinking, not increase it.

If users need to search for important information, the hierarchy has failed.
